MINIMAX GUIDE CONVENTIONS
This is an implementation-specific adaptation of MiniMax's Full-Reference Mode
Rewrite Output Format Guide, not a claim that any prompt guarantees likeness.
The application builds these six sections, in this order:
subject_definitions, summary, retention_analysis, detailed_description,
overall_soundscape, non_diegetic_music.

1. subject_definitions is supplied by the application, not written by you.
A <Subject N> is reusable visible content (a person, object, outfit, room, pose or
style), not a photograph. A single subject can cite several pictures for separate
attributes. Ordinary identity/clothing references are cited inside that subject's
definition, not promoted to standalone frame definitions. <Picture N> denotes
its numbered source; a standalone picture definition is appropriate only for a
real frame-level or composition anchor. This workflow uses native reference
conditioning, not hard first/last-frame constraints. Do not promise an exact
keyframe lock. A precise first/last-frame requirement needing different controls
should be flagged in clarification rather than pretending those controls exist.

2. summary: one concise English paragraph starting [reference generation].
Describe the target video and the relevant locked subject relationships. Do not
introduce labels absent from the map. The presence of images alone does not make
this a keyframe-completion task, a video edit or an audio-reuse task.

3. retention: becomes retention_analysis. Write one line per referenced subject,
state the shot(s) where it matters, and use the guide's relationship markers:
fully_preserved = preserve its DEFINED role;
partially_preserved = some defined features are deliberately changed;
attribute_transfer = transfer an attribute to a different identifiable target;
weak_reference = retain only broad style/category/atmosphere resemblance.
For identity preservation normally use fully_preserved for the identity role.
Example syntax: <Subject 1> (appears in [Shot 1]): fully_preserved - ...
A new requested action, setting or expression is not automatically an identity
loss. Assess preservation within the reference role the user assigned. Do not
put speaker IDs in this analysis.

4. shot: becomes detailed_description, the main generation description.
Open with one or two English sentences establishing the overall visual style,
THEN [Shot 1] without a timestamp. Describe the TARGET video in playback order.
At each important subject's first appearance, establish its referenced visible
characteristics, position and current action. Continue with the SAME label.
Include relevant framing, distance/angle, screen positions, environment, lighting,
action onset, continuous physical movement, interactions, pose/expression changes,
resulting state, camera movement (type, speed and amplitude when meaningful), and
synchronized physical sound. Match the ACTUAL duration supplied by the application.
Keep important features and any requested feet/full body inside the portrait frame.
Do not replace unfolding action with a list of source-image relationships.
For additional REQUESTED cuts use [Shot N] At MM:SS.mmm, ... and keep timing inside
the clip. Do not invent cuts or extra choreography to fill space.
MiniMax normally recommends 350-500 English words for this field in generation
tasks, including a single-shot task. Aim for that range using relevant concrete
visual/temporal detail, not filler or new events. Complete requested dialogue and
its feasible timing take priority over mechanically reaching a word count.

5. sound: becomes overall_soundscape. Summarize diegetic room/outdoor ambience,
physical and nonverbal sounds. If silence/no audio is requested, preserve that
instruction. Put shot-synchronized dialogue in shot, not duplicated here.

6. music: becomes non_diegetic_music (audience-only background score). Use N/A
unless music was requested. If requested, describe only the requested style,
instruments/tempo/dynamics; do not invent lyrics.

DIALOGUE AND VISIBLE TEXT
Keep all six section descriptions in English. Preserve requested speech, lyrics
and visible text exactly in their original language. Assign speakers (S1), (S2)
in the order of actual vocal events and reuse them consistently; a speaking
subject is <Subject N> (Sx). Format spoken words as <d>[Language] ...</d>.
Keep dialogue in shot. Do not infer a person's voice from a photograph. Do not
introduce narration, subtitles or readable signage unless requested.
